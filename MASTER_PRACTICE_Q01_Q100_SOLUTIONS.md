### Question 1 (Easy)

**Question:**
Alpha purchases a newly built production line from Beta. Instead of paying cash, Alpha issues new bonds directly to Beta in payment. A week later, Beta sells those bonds to Gamma for cash. Alpha continues to own and use the production line.

a. For each transaction, identify the real asset and financial claims involved. State who holds the bonds and who owes the bond payments after each transaction.
b. Beta describes the second transaction as “another round of financing for Alpha.” Assess this description.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Real Assets*: Physical productive assets that generate goods, services, and net income (e.g., equipment, machinery).
- *Financial Assets / Claims*: Contractual claims allocating future cash flows generated by real assets (e.g., bonds, equities).
- *Primary Market*: Issuance of new securities where the issuer receives capital/financing.
- *Secondary Market*: Trading of existing securities between investors; issuer receives no new funds.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a):**
  - **Transaction 1 (Alpha issues bonds to Beta for production line):**
    - *Real Asset*: The newly built production line (transferred from Beta to Alpha).
    - *Financial Claim*: The newly issued bonds (debt contract created by Alpha and given to Beta).
    - *Holders & Obligors*: Alpha owes the bond payments (issuer/borrower); Beta holds the bonds (investor/lender).
  - **Transaction 2 (Beta sells bonds to Gamma for cash):**
    - *Real Asset*: None. The production line remains with Alpha and is not part of this trade.
    - *Financial Claims*: The existing Alpha bonds (transferred from Beta to Gamma) and cash (transferred from Gamma to Beta).
    - *Holders & Obligors*: Alpha still owes the bond payments (its contractual obligation does not change); Gamma now holds the bonds (new creditor); Beta holds cash.
- **Part (b): Assessment of Beta's statement:**
  - Beta's description is **incorrect**.
  - The second transaction is a **secondary market transaction** between Beta and Gamma. Alpha is neither a party to this transaction nor does it receive any new cash or capital.
  - The first transaction was the primary market financing (Alpha financed the capital expenditure by issuing debt). The second transaction merely reallocates the existing claim from Beta to Gamma, providing liquidity to Beta, not new financing to Alpha.

### Question 2 (Easy)

**Question:**
Delta has issued ordinary bonds, preferred shares and common shares. An investor makes these statements:

| Statement | Claim |
|---|---|
| A | Preferred shareholders rank ahead of common shareholders, so they must also rank ahead of bondholders. |
| B | A stated preferred dividend is a contractual debt payment; omitting it has the same consequence as failing to pay a bond coupon. |
| C | Common shareholders have limited liability, so they cannot lose the money they paid for their shares. |

Assess each statement and give a corrected explanation. Assume ordinary securities with no special contractual provisions; detailed insolvency rules are not required.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Priority of Claims (Capital Structure)*: Senior Debt $\rightarrow$ Subordinated Debt $\rightarrow$ Preferred Stock $\rightarrow$ Common Stock.
- *Debt vs. Equity*: Debt interest/coupon is a legal contractual obligation (failure leads to default/bankruptcy). Dividends are discretionary distributions authorized by the board of directors.
- *Limited Liability*: Stockholders' losses are capped at their investment; personal assets are protected.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Statement A:** **Incorrect.**
  - *Correction*: Preferred shareholders rank ahead of *common* shareholders, but they rank **behind (junior/subordinated to) all bondholders and debt holders**. In liquidation or bankruptcy, creditors (bondholders) must be paid in full before preferred shareholders receive anything.
- **Statement B:** **Incorrect.**
  - *Correction*: Preferred dividends are **equity distributions**, not contractual debt payments. The omission of a preferred dividend does **not** constitute a legal default and cannot force the firm into bankruptcy. While preferred dividends are typically cumulative (unpaid dividends accumulate and must be paid before common stock dividends), omitting them carries no immediate bankruptcy consequences, whereas missing a bond coupon is an immediate event of default.
- **Statement C:** **Incorrect.**
  - *Correction*: Limited liability protects common shareholders' **personal wealth outside the company** (they cannot be held liable for the company's debts beyond their initial investment). However, they **can lose up to 100% of the money they paid for their shares** if the firm's equity value falls to zero.

### Question 3 (Easy)

**Question:**
Two managers describe their investment decisions:

| Manager | Decision |
|---|---|
| Epsilon | After reviewing inflation and economic growth forecasts, reduces the portfolio's equity allocation from 60% to 40%. Epsilon then chooses individual bonds by comparing issuers' credit quality and yields. |
| Zeta | Finds Eta's shares attractively priced after studying the firm's cash flows and competitive position. Zeta buys the shares despite an unfavourable industry outlook. |

a. Classify each manager's investment process and identify the asset-allocation or security-selection decisions.
b. Epsilon claims the strategy is passive because the portfolio holds many securities. Is that sufficient to support the claim?

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Top-Down Investing*: Starts with macroeconomic analysis $\rightarrow$ Asset Allocation $\rightarrow$ Security Selection within asset classes.
- *Bottom-Up Investing*: Starts directly with security-level fundamental analysis and stock picking, ignoring or de-emphasizing macro/industry conditions.
- *Active vs. Passive Management*: Passive management tracks an established index/benchmark with rule-based weights. Active management involves market timing, changing weights based on forecasts, or picking mispriced securities.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a):**
  - **Manager Epsilon**:
    - *Process*: **Top-Down**.
    - *Asset Allocation*: Adjusting broad asset weights (reducing equity from 60% to 40%, increasing bonds from 40% to 60%) based on macroeconomic forecasts of inflation and GDP growth.
    - *Security Selection*: Selecting specific bonds based on credit quality and yield comparisons.
  - **Manager Zeta**:
    - *Process*: **Bottom-Up**.
    - *Security Selection*: Selecting Eta shares based on micro-level firm fundamentals (cash flows, competitive position) despite a negative industry outlook. No asset allocation decision is made.
- **Part (b):**
  - Epsilon's claim is **incorrect/unsupported**.
  - Holding many securities is merely **diversification**, not passivity.
  - Epsilon actively adjusted broad asset class weights based on macro forecasts (tactical asset allocation / market timing) and actively selected individual bonds using credit analysis. Both decisions are active management. A passive strategy would hold a fixed index benchmark without discretionary adjustments based on forecasts.

### Question 4 (Average)

**Question:**
Four investors take separate positions on Theta shares. All contracts expire on the same date.

| Investor | Position | Strike or delivery price per share |
|---|---|---:|
| Iota | Buys a put option | USD 50 |
| Kappa | Enters a short forward | USD 50 |
| Lambda | Buys a call option | USD 50 |
| Mu | Enters a long forward | USD 50 |

Option premiums are paid when the positions are opened. Assume no counterparty default.

a. At expiration, Theta trades at USD 62. Should Iota exercise the put? Can Kappa choose not to deliver?
b. Consider the alternative outcome in which Theta instead trades at USD 38. Should Lambda exercise the call? Can Mu decline to buy?
c. An adviser says, “An option buyer can walk away, so an option that is not exercised costs the buyer nothing.” Assess the statement. No profit calculations are required.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Call Payoff*: $\max(0, S_T - X)$. Option to buy at $X$. Exercise if $S_T > X$.
- *Put Payoff*: $\max(0, X - S_T)$. Option to sell at $X$. Exercise if $S_T < X$.
- *Forward Contract*: Firm legal **obligation** for both parties. Long forward payoff: $S_T - F$; Short forward payoff: $F - S_T$. Neither party can walk away.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Terminal Price $S_T = 62$ USD:**
  - *Iota (Long Put at 50)*: Put payoff is $\max(0, 50 - 62) = 0$. Selling at 50 when the market price is 62 makes no sense. Iota **should NOT exercise**.
  - *Kappa (Short Forward at 50)*: A forward contract is an unconditional legal obligation, not an option. Kappa **CANNOT choose not to deliver**; Kappa is legally bound to deliver/settle at 50 (incurring a loss of $50 - 62 = -12$ USD).
- **Part (b): Terminal Price $S_T = 38$ USD:**
  - *Lambda (Long Call at 50)*: Call payoff is $\max(0, 38 - 50) = 0$. Buying at 50 when the market price is 38 makes no sense. Lambda **should NOT exercise**.
  - *Mu (Long Forward at 50)*: A forward contract is a binding commitment. Mu **CANNOT decline to buy**; Mu is legally obligated to buy at 50 (incurring a loss of $38 - 50 = -12$ USD).
- **Part (c): Assessment of the Adviser's statement:**
  - The statement is **misleading and false**.
  - While it is true that an unexercised option creates no *further* obligations or cash outflows at expiration (payoff is zero, not negative), it did **not** cost nothing: the buyer paid an upfront **option premium** at inception. If the option expires unexercised, the entire initial premium is lost (Net Profit $= -\text{Premium}$).

### Question 5 (Average)

**Question:**
Nu sells Treasury securities to Xi for USD 240,000 today and agrees to repurchase the same securities from Xi for USD 241,080 after 45 days. No coupon is paid during the agreement, no fees apply, and both parties perform as agreed.

a. Identify the cash borrower and lender. Which party describes its position as a repo, and which as a reverse repo?
b. Show the cash flows for each party today and on day 45. Calculate the lender's financing income.
c. Calculate the lender's 45-day return and its simple annualized rate on a 360-day basis.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Repurchase Agreement (Repo)*: Sale of securities coupled with a simultaneous agreement to buy them back later at a higher price. It is economically a secured collateralized cash loan.
- *Party Terminology*:
  - The seller of securities (cash borrower) does a **Repo**.
  - The buyer of securities (cash lender) does a **Reverse Repo**.
- *Money Market Simple Annualized Yield (360-day convention)*:
  $$\text{Simple Annual Rate} = \text{Holding Period Return} \times \frac{360}{\text{Days}}$$

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a):**
  - **Cash Borrower**: **Nu** (receives cash today, repays principal + interest on day 45).
  - **Cash Lender**: **Xi** (provides cash today, receives repayment on day 45).
  - **Terminology**: Nu describes its trade as a **Repo** (selling securities with agreement to repurchase). Xi describes its trade as a **Reverse Repo** (buying securities with agreement to resell).
- **Part (b): Cash Flow Schedule & Financing Income:**
  - *Today ($t=0$)*:
    - Nu: $+240{,}000$ USD
    - Xi: $-240{,}000$ USD
  - *Day 45 ($t=45$)*:
    - Nu: $-241{,}080$ USD
    - Xi: $+241{,}080$ USD
  - *Lender's (Xi's) Financing Income*:
    $$\text{Income} = 241{,}080 - 240{,}000 = 1{,}080 \text{ USD}$$
- **Part (c): 45-Day Return & Simple Annualized Rate:**
  - *45-day holding-period return*:
    $$R_{45} = \frac{241{,}080 - 240{,}000}{240{,}000} = \frac{1{,}080}{240{,}000} = 0.0045 = 0.45\%$$
  - *Simple annualized rate (360-day year)*:
    $$\text{Annual Rate} = 0.45\% \times \frac{360}{45} = 0.45\% \times 8 = 3.60\%$$

### Question 6 (Average)

**Question:**
Two options on Omicron shares expire in one month:

| Purchased option | Strike price per share | Premium per share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Call | USD 46 | USD 3.50 |
| Put | USD 50 | USD 2.25 |

Consider each purchase separately. The options can be exercised only at expiration.

a. For terminal share prices of USD 42, USD 48 and USD 56, calculate each option's payoff and its buyer's net profit per share.
b. Find each buyer's break-even terminal share price.
c. At USD 48, what should each option holder do? Explain.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Call Payoff*: $\max(0, S_T - X)$, Net Profit: $\text{Payoff} - C_0$. Break-even: $S_T = X + C_0$.
- *Put Payoff*: $\max(0, X - S_T)$, Net Profit: $\text{Payoff} - P_0$. Break-even: $S_T = X - P_0$.
- *Expiration Exercise Rule*: The initial premium is a **sunk cost**. At $T$, exercise whenever $\text{Payoff} > 0$, even if net profit is negative!

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Payoff and Net Profit Schedule:**
  - *At $S_T = 42$:*
    - **Call ($X=46$)**: Payoff $= \max(0, 42 - 46) = 0$. Net Profit $= 0 - 3.50 = -3.50$ USD.
    - **Put ($X=50$)**: Payoff $= \max(0, 50 - 42) = 8.00$ USD. Net Profit $= 8.00 - 2.25 = +5.75$ USD.
  - *At $S_T = 48$:*
    - **Call ($X=46$)**: Payoff $= \max(0, 48 - 46) = 2.00$ USD. Net Profit $= 2.00 - 3.50 = -1.50$ USD.
    - **Put ($X=50$)**: Payoff $= \max(0, 50 - 48) = 2.00$ USD. Net Profit $= 2.00 - 2.25 = -0.25$ USD.
  - *At $S_T = 56$:*
    - **Call ($X=46$)**: Payoff $= \max(0, 56 - 46) = 10.00$ USD. Net Profit $= 10.00 - 3.50 = +6.50$ USD.
    - **Put ($X=50$)**: Payoff $= \max(0, 50 - 56) = 0$. Net Profit $= 0 - 2.25 = -2.25$ USD.
- **Part (b): Break-Even Terminal Prices:**
  - *Call Break-Even*: $S_{\text{BE}} = X + C_0 = 46 + 3.50 = 49.50$ USD.
  - *Put Break-Even*: $S_{\text{BE}} = X - P_0 = 50 - 2.25 = 47.75$ USD.
- **Part (c): Action at $S_T = 48$ USD:**
  - **Call Holder**: **Exercise**. Payoff is $48 - 46 = 2.00$ USD. Exercising recovers $2.00$ USD of the $3.50$ USD premium, yielding a net loss of $-1.50$ USD instead of $-3.50$ USD if abandoned.
  - **Put Holder**: **Exercise**. Payoff is $50 - 48 = 2.00$ USD. Exercising recovers $2.00$ USD of the $2.25$ USD premium, yielding a net loss of $-0.25$ USD instead of $-2.25$ USD if abandoned.
  - *Core principle*: Option premiums are sunk costs. At maturity, an investor exercises whenever the option is in-the-money (payoff $> 0$) to minimize loss.

### Question 7 (Average)

**Question:**
Pi bought a call option on Rho shares. The strike price and premium are missing from the record. A payoff worksheet shows the following possible expiration outcomes for that same option:

| Terminal share price (USD) | Buyer's net profit per share (USD) |
|---:|---:|
| 47 | −4 |
| 59 | +3 |
| 68 | +12 |

The worksheet includes the premium but no other costs.

a. Recover the premium and strike price.
b. Check your answer against all three rows. Calculate the payoff and net profit at a terminal share price of USD 54, and state whether Pi should exercise.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- Call profit function: $\text{Profit}(S_T) = \max(0, S_T - X) - \text{Premium}$.
- In the in-the-money region ($S_T > X$), $\frac{\Delta \text{Profit}}{\Delta S_T} = 1.0$.
- In the out-of-the-money region ($S_T \le X$), Payoff $= 0$, so $\text{Profit} = -\text{Premium}$.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Recover Premium and Strike Price:**
  1. Observe the slope between $S_T = 59$ and $S_T = 68$:
     $$\frac{\Delta \text{Profit}}{\Delta S_T} = \frac{12 - 3}{68 - 59} = \frac{9}{9} = 1.0$$
     Because the slope is $1.0$, the option is in-the-money at both 59 and 68.
  2. For an in-the-money call:
     $$\text{Profit} = S_T - X - \text{Premium}$$
     At $S_T = 59$:
     $$3 = 59 - (X + \text{Premium}) \implies X + \text{Premium} = 56$$
  3. Now examine $S_T = 47$ where Profit is $-4$:
     If the option is out-of-the-money at 47 (Payoff $= 0$), then $\text{Profit} = -\text{Premium} = -4 \implies \mathbf{\text{Premium} = 4\text{ USD}}$.
  4. Solving for the strike price:
     $$X + 4 = 56 \implies \mathbf{X = 52\text{ USD}}$$
- **Part (b): Verification & Analysis at $S_T = 54$:**
  - *Verification of rows*:
    - $S_T = 47$: $\max(0, 47 - 52) - 4 = 0 - 4 = -4$. (Matches!)
    - $S_T = 59$: $\max(0, 59 - 52) - 4 = 7 - 4 = +3$. (Matches!)
    - $S_T = 68$: $\max(0, 68 - 52) - 4 = 16 - 4 = +12$. (Matches!)
  - *At $S_T = 54$:*
    - Payoff $= \max(0, 54 - 52) = 2.00$ USD.
    - Net Profit $= 2.00 - 4.00 = -2.00$ USD.
    - **Action**: Pi **should exercise** because Payoff $= 2.00 > 0$. Exercising reduces the loss from $-4.00$ USD to $-2.00$ USD.

### Question 8 (Hard)

**Question:**
Sigma has USD 10,000 to invest for one year. Tau shares currently cost USD 50 and pay no dividends. Sigma compares two fixed strategies:

| Strategy | Investment today |
|---|---|
| Shares | Buy 200 Tau shares. |
| Calls and cash | Buy 10 call contracts, each covering 100 Tau shares, at a premium of USD 5 per share. Invest the remaining money at a guaranteed annual return of 4%. |

The calls have a strike price of USD 55 and expire in one year. The contracts are cash-settled at expiration. No borrowing is allowed.

Sigma uses the following forecasts for Tau's price in one year:

| State | Probability | Terminal share price (USD) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Weak | 0.20 | 35 |
| Moderate | 0.40 | 55 |
| Strong | 0.40 | 75 |

a. Verify each strategy's initial cost and calculate the cash balance at the end of the year.
b. Calculate each strategy's terminal wealth and one-year return in every state.
c. Calculate each strategy's expected return and expected terminal wealth.
d. Sigma plans to select whichever strategy has the higher expected return. Assess this decision using your results.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Options Leverage*: Call options provide asymmetric upside leverage with downside loss limited to premium paid.
- *Expected Value & Return*: $E[W] = \sum p_s W_s$, $E[R] = \sum p_s R_s$.
- *Risk-Return Tradeoff*: Higher expected return comes with higher volatility and substantial downside risk; selecting solely on expected return violates basic finance principles.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Initial Cost & Ending Cash Balance:**
  - **Strategy 1 (Shares)**:
    - Buys 200 shares at $50 = 10{,}000$ USD.
    - Cash invested $= 0$; ending cash balance $= 0$.
  - **Strategy 2 (Calls and Cash)**:
    - 10 contracts $\times 100$ shares/contract $= 1{,}000$ underlying shares.
    - Premium paid $= 1{,}000 \times 5 = 5{,}000$ USD.
    - Remaining cash invested $= 10{,}000 - 5{,}000 = 5{,}000$ USD.
    - Guaranteed return $= 4\%$. Ending cash balance $= 5{,}000 \times (1 + 0.04) = \mathbf{5{,}200\text{ USD}}$.
- **Part (b): Terminal Wealth and One-Year Return by State:**
  - *Weak State ($S_T = 35$, Prob $= 0.20$):*
    - Strategy 1: Wealth $= 200 \times 35 = 7{,}000$ USD. Return $= \frac{7{,}000 - 10{,}000}{10{,}000} = \mathbf{-30.0\%}$.
    - Strategy 2: Call payoff $= 1{,}000 \times \max(0, 35 - 55) = 0$. Wealth $= 0 + 5{,}200 = 5{,}200$ USD. Return $= \frac{5{,}200 - 10{,}000}{10{,}000} = \mathbf{-48.0\%}$.
  - *Moderate State ($S_T = 55$, Prob $= 0.40$):*
    - Strategy 1: Wealth $= 200 \times 55 = 11{,}000$ USD. Return $= \frac{11{,}000 - 10{,}000}{10{,}000} = \mathbf{+10.0\%}$.
    - Strategy 2: Call payoff $= 1{,}000 \times \max(0, 55 - 55) = 0$. Wealth $= 0 + 5{,}200 = 5{,}200$ USD. Return $= \frac{5{,}200 - 10{,}000}{10{,}000} = \mathbf{-48.0\%}$.
  - *Strong State ($S_T = 75$, Prob $= 0.40$):*
    - Strategy 1: Wealth $= 200 \times 75 = 15{,}000$ USD. Return $= \frac{15{,}000 - 10{,}000}{10{,}000} = \mathbf{+50.0\%}$.
    - Strategy 2: Call payoff $= 1{,}000 \times (75 - 55) = 20{,}000$ USD. Wealth $= 20{,}000 + 5{,}200 = 25{,}200$ USD. Return $= \frac{25{,}200 - 10{,}000}{10{,}000} = \mathbf{+152.0\%}$.
- **Part (c): Expected Terminal Wealth & Expected Return:**
  - **Strategy 1 (Shares)**:
    $$E[W_1] = 0.20(7{,}000) + 0.40(11{,}000) + 0.40(15{,}000) = 1{,}400 + 4{,}400 + 6{,}000 = \mathbf{11{,}800\text{ USD}}$$
    $$E[R_1] = 0.20(-30\%) + 0.40(10\%) + 0.40(50\%) = -6\% + 4\% + 20\% = \mathbf{+18.0\%}$$
  - **Strategy 2 (Calls and Cash)**:
    $$E[W_2] = 0.20(5{,}200) + 0.40(5{,}200) + 0.40(25{,}200) = 0.60(5{,}200) + 0.40(25{,}200) = 3{,}120 + 10{,}080 = \mathbf{13{,}200\text{ USD}}$$
    $$E[R_2] = 0.60(-48\%) + 0.40(152\%) = -28.8\% + 60.8\% = \mathbf{+32.0\%}$$
- **Part (d): Assessment of Sigma's Decision Rule:**
  - Selecting Strategy 2 solely because $E[R_2] = 32\% > E[R_1] = 18\%$ is **flawed and dangerous**.
  - Strategy 2 incurs **extreme downside risk**: in $60\%$ of the states (Weak and Moderate), the call options expire worthless, resulting in a severe **$-48\%$ loss** (wealth drops to $5{,}200$ USD).
  - Strategy 1 protects wealth far better in moderate conditions ($+10\%$ gain) and has a milder loss in the weak state ($-30\%$).

- *Yield to Maturity (YTM)*: IRR assuming the bond is held to final maturity $T$ at face value:
  $$P = \sum_{t=1}^{T} \frac{C}{(1+\text{YTM})^t} + \frac{\text{Face}}{(1+\text{YTM})^T}$$

- *Liquidity Preference Theory*: $f_{t-1,t} = E(r_{t-1,t}) + LP_t \implies E(r_{t-1,t}) = f_{t-1,t} - LP_t$.
- The implied forward rates are objectively determined by the market spot curve; liquidity premiums are subjective modeling assumptions.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Expected Short Rates:**
  - Implied forward rates from spot curve:
    $$1 + f_{1,2} = \frac{(1.038)^2}{1.030} = \frac{1.077444}{1.030} = 1.046062 \implies f_{1,2} = 4.6062\%$$
    $$1 + f_{2,3} = \frac{(1.045)^3}{(1.038)^2} = \frac{1.141166}{1.077444} = 1.059142 \implies f_{2,3} = 5.9142\%$$
  - Expected short rates under Sigma's liquidity premiums:
    $$E(r_{1,2}) = 4.6062\% - 0.80\% = \mathbf{3.8062\%}$$
    $$E(r_{2,3}) = 5.9142\% - 1.20\% = \mathbf{4.7142\%}$$
- **Part (b): Tau's Expected Short Rate & What Changes vs. Stays Fixed:**
  - Tau's expected Year 3 rate:
    $$E(r_{2,3}) = 5.9142\% - 1.80\% = \mathbf{4.1142\%}$$
  - **What stays fixed**: The market spot rates and the curve-implied forward rate $f_{2,3} = 5.9142\%$ are directly observed in the market and do not change.
  - **What changes**: The subjective decomposition between expected interest rates and liquidity risk compensation. Assigning a larger liquidity premium ($1.8\%$ vs $1.2\%$) reduces the inferred expected future short rate ($4.1142\%$ vs $4.7142\%$).

**Question:**
Upsilon and Phi offer explanations for the observed default-free spot curve below (spot rates 1 to 5 years: 3.0%, 3.6%, 4.1%, 4.4%, 4.6%).

- *Liquidity Preference Theory (LPT)*: Forward rates equal expected rates plus positive liquidity premiums ($LP > 0$). An upward-sloping curve can occur even if expected rates are flat or declining, provided liquidity premiums increase with maturity.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Does the curve rule out either explanation?**
  - **No.** Both explanations are mathematically and economically consistent with the observed upward-sloping spot curve.
- **Does the curve establish either one?**
  - **No.** The spot curve alone gives only a single observable number for each forward rate ($f_{t-1,t}$). Because $f_{t-1,t} = E(r_t) + LP_t$ has one known value and two unknowns, the observed curve **cannot identify or prove** how much of the slope is due to expectations versus liquidity premiums without additional external data or structural assumptions.

### Question 22 (Hard)

**Question:**
The default-free one- and two-year spot rates are 3% and 4%. Chi is a default-free three-year bond with face value 1,000 and annual coupons of 40. Its curve-consistent price is 980.
Psi has the same maturity and default treatment, but pays annual coupons of 25 and repays 1,000 at maturity.
a. Find the missing three-year spot rate.
b. Find Psi's curve-consistent price.
c. Omega proposes discounting every Psi payment at the three-year spot rate. Calculate the resulting price and assess this method.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Bootstrapping Spot Rates*: Using the market price of a coupon bond and known spot rates to solve for the missing spot rate.
- *Law of One Price*: Cash flows occurring at the same time must be discounted at the same spot rate.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Find the 3-Year Spot Rate ($s_3$):**
  - Chi has cash flows: $C_1 = 40, C_2 = 40, C_3 = 1{,}040$. Price $= 980$.
    $$980 = \frac{40}{1.03} + \frac{40}{(1.04)^2} + \frac{1{,}040}{(1+s_3)^3}$$
    $$980 = 38.8350 + 36.9822 + \frac{1{,}040}{(1+s_3)^3} = 75.8172 + \frac{1{,}040}{(1+s_3)^3}$$
    $$\frac{1{,}040}{(1+s_3)^3} = 980 - 75.8172 = 904.1828$$
    $$(1+s_3)^3 = \frac{1{,}040}{904.1828} = 1.150210 \implies 1 + s_3 = 1.047753 \implies \mathbf{s_3 = 4.7753\%}$$
- **Part (b): Psi's Curve-Consistent Price:**
  - Psi cash flows: $C_1 = 25, C_2 = 25, C_3 = 1{,}025$.
    $$P_{\text{Psi}} = \frac{25}{1.03} + \frac{25}{(1.04)^2} + \frac{1{,}025}{(1.047753)^3}$$
    $$P_{\text{Psi}} = 24.2718 + 23.1139 + \frac{1{,}025}{1.150210} = 24.2718 + 23.1139 + 891.1418 = \mathbf{938.53\text{ USD}}$$
- **Part (c): Omega's Method (Single Discount Rate $s_3$):**
  - Omega discounts all cash flows at $s_3 = 4.7753\%$:
    $$P_{\text{Omega}} = \frac{25}{1.047753} + \frac{25}{(1.047753)^2} + \frac{1{,}025}{(1.047753)^3} = 23.8606 + 22.7731 + 891.1418 = \mathbf{937.78\text{ USD}}$$
  - **Assessment**: Omega's method is **flawed and incorrect**. It treats the coupon bond as if all payments were subject to the 3-year spot rate. Because the spot curve is upward-sloping ($s_1 < s_2 < s_3$), Omega discounts the Year 1 and Year 2 cash flows at a rate that is too high ($4.7753\%$ instead of $3\%$ and $4\%$), underpricing the bond ($937.78$ vs true value $938.53$).

### Question 23 (Hard)

**Question:**
Alpha holds a default-free three-year bond with face value 10,000 and a 6% annual coupon. Its clean quote is 102.60 per 100 of face value. The next coupon is in one year.
Default-free zero-coupon bonds are available at the prices below:

| Zero maturity | Price per 100 of face value |
|---|---:|
| 1 year | 96.00 |
| 2 years | 91.00 |
| 3 years | 85.50 |

a. Specify the face amount of each zero needed to reproduce the coupon bond's payments. Show the payment at every date for both alternatives.
b. Compare the cost of this zero portfolio with the quoted value of Alpha's holding.
c. Assess the consistency of the coupon bond's quote with the zero prices. What quote per 100 would be consistent?

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Replication*: Any coupon bond can be perfectly replicated by a portfolio of zero-coupon bonds matching each coupon and principal cash flow date.
- *Law of One Price & Arbitrage*: Price of coupon bond must equal total cost of replicating zeros.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Replicating Portfolio:**
  - Alpha's bond cash flows on $10{,}000$ face at $6\%$ coupon:
    - Year 1: $600$ USD
    - Year 2: $600$ USD
    - Year 3: $10{,}600$ USD
  - Replicating Face Amounts:
    - **1-Year Zero**: Face amount $= \mathbf{600\text{ USD}}$ (pays $600$ at $t=1$).
    - **2-Year Zero**: Face amount $= \mathbf{600\text{ USD}}$ (pays $600$ at $t=2$).
    - **3-Year Zero**: Face amount $= \mathbf{10{,}600\text{ USD}}$ (pays $10{,}600$ at $t=3$).
- **Part (b): Cost Comparison:**
  - Cost of replicating portfolio:
    $$\text{Cost} = 600 \times 0.9600 + 600 \times 0.9100 + 10{,}600 \times 0.8550$$
    $$\text{Cost} = 576.00 + 546.00 + 9{,}063.00 = \mathbf{10{,}185.00\text{ USD}}$$
  - Quoted market value of Alpha's bond:
    $$\text{Quoted Value} = 10{,}000 \times \frac{102.60}{100} = \mathbf{10{,}260.00\text{ USD}}$$
  - The coupon bond is quoted at $10{,}260.00$ USD, which is **$75.00$ USD more expensive** than the identical synthetic zero portfolio ($10{,}185.00$ USD).
- **Part (c): Assessment & Consistent Quote:**
  - The quote is **inconsistent** with no-arbitrage pricing (the coupon bond is overpriced).
  - *Arbitrage strategy*: Short-sell the coupon bond for $+10{,}260$ USD, buy the replicating zeros for $-10{,}185$ USD. All future cash outflows are completely matched by cash inflows from the zeros, locking in an immediate riskless arbitrage profit of $+75.00$ USD.
  - The curve-consistent quote per 100 face value is:
    $$\text{Quote} = \frac{10{,}185.00}{10{,}000} \times 100 = \mathbf{101.85}$$

### Question 24 (Hard)

**Question:**
Beta buys a three-year bond with face value 1,000 and a 6% annual coupon when its YTM is 5%. One year later, Beta receives the coupon and immediately sells the bond. The sale price excludes the coupon just received. At that date the bond's YTM has the following distribution:

| State | Probability | YTM at sale |
|---|---:|---:|
| Lower yields | 0.25 | 3% |
| Unchanged yields | 0.50 | 5% |
| Higher yields | 0.25 | 8% |

a. Find the purchase price.
b. Find the sale price and one-year holding-period return in each state.
c. Calculate the expected holding-period return and its standard deviation.
d. Assess the claim: “Buying at a 5% YTM makes Beta's one-year return 5%, regardless of the sale-date yield.”

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Holding Period Return (HPR)*:
  $$\text{HPR} = \frac{P_1 + C - P_0}{P_0}$$
- *One Year Later*: The bond has 2 years remaining to maturity. Price is $P_1(y) = \frac{60}{1+y} + \frac{1{,}060}{(1+y)^2}$.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Purchase Price ($P_0$):**
  $$P_0 = \frac{60}{1.05} + \frac{60}{(1.05)^2} + \frac{1{,}060}{(1.05)^3} = 57.1429 + 54.4218 + 915.6678 = \mathbf{1{,}027.23\text{ USD}}$$
- **Part (b): Sale Price and HPR by State:**
  - *State 1 ($y = 3\%$, prob $= 0.25$):*
    $$P_1 = \frac{60}{1.03} + \frac{1{,}060}{(1.03)^2} = 58.2524 + 999.1517 = 1{,}057.4041\text{ USD}$$
    $$\text{HPR}_1 = \frac{1{,}057.4041 + 60 - 1{,}027.2325}{1{,}027.2325} = \frac{90.1716}{1{,}027.2325} = \mathbf{+8.7781\%}$$
  - *State 2 ($y = 5\%$, prob $= 0.50$):*
    $$P_1 = \frac{60}{1.05} + \frac{1{,}060}{(1.05)^2} = 57.1429 + 961.4512 = 1{,}018.5941\text{ USD}$$
    $$\text{HPR}_2 = \frac{1{,}018.5941 + 60 - 1{,}027.2325}{1{,}027.2325} = \frac{51.3616}{1{,}027.2325} = \mathbf{+5.0000\%}$$
  - *State 3 ($y = 8\%$, prob $= 0.25$):*
    $$P_1 = \frac{60}{1.08} + \frac{1{,}060}{(1.08)^2} = 55.5556 + 908.7791 = 964.3347\text{ USD}$$
    $$\text{HPR}_3 = \frac{964.3347 + 60 - 1{,}027.2325}{1{,}027.2325} = \frac{-2.8978}{1{,}027.2325} = \mathbf{-0.2821\%}$$
- **Part (c): Expected Return and Standard Deviation:**
  - *Expected Return*:
    $$E[\text{HPR}] = 0.25(8.7781\%) + 0.50(5.0000\%) + 0.25(-0.2821\%) = 2.1945 + 2.5000 - 0.0705 = \mathbf{4.6240\%}$$
  - *Variance & SD*:
    $$\sigma^2 = 0.25(8.7781 - 4.6240)^2 + 0.50(5.0000 - 4.6240)^2 + 0.25(-0.2821 - 4.6240)^2$$
    $$\sigma^2 = 0.25(4.1541)^2 + 0.50(0.3760)^2 + 0.25(-4.9061)^2 = 4.3141 + 0.0707 + 6.0175 = 10.4023$$
    $$\text{SD} = \sqrt{10.4023} = \mathbf{3.2253\%}$$
- **Part (d): Assessment of Claim:**
  - The claim is **entirely false**.
  - A bond's 1-year holding period return equals its purchase YTM ($5\%$) **only if the yield remains unchanged at 5%**.
  - If the market yield rises to $8\%$, capital losses drag the return down to $-0.28\%$. If the yield falls to $3\%$, capital gains boost the return to $+8.78\%$. A bond's market price fluctuates with interest rates, exposing the investor to significant **price risk / interest rate risk**.

**Question:**
Lambda's option-free bond has: YTM $= 5.5\%$, Price $= 873.308680$, Modified duration $= 6.673273$ years, Annual convexity $= 54.476335$ years$^2$.
Its repriced values are $966.336276$ at a 4% yield ($\Delta y = -0.015$) and $791.004552$ at a 7% yield ($\Delta y = +0.015$).
a. Estimate the price at each new yield using duration alone and then duration plus convexity.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a) & (b): Calculations and Errors:**
  - **Scenario 1: Yield falls to 4% ($\Delta y = -0.015$, Exact $= 966.3363$ USD)**:
    - *Duration Only*:
      $$\hat{P}_{\text{dur}} = 873.308680 \times [1 - 6.673273(-0.015)] = 873.308680 \times 1.100099 = \mathbf{960.7261\text{ USD}}$$
      $$\text{Error} = 960.7261 - 966.3363 = \mathbf{-5.6102\text{ USD}} \quad \left(\frac{-5.6102}{873.3087} = \mathbf{-0.64\%}\right)$$
    - *Duration + Convexity*:
      $$\text{Conv Term} = 0.5 \times 54.476335 \times (-0.015)^2 = 0.0061286$$
      $$\hat{P}_{\text{D+C}} = 873.308680 \times [1 + 0.100099 + 0.0061286] = \mathbf{966.0782\text{ USD}}$$

    - *Duration Only*:
      $$\hat{P}_{\text{dur}} = 873.308680 \times [1 - 6.673273(+0.015)] = 873.308680 \times 0.899901 = \mathbf{785.8913\text{ USD}}$$

Therefore, a linear duration estimate always underpredicts the true price for both yield declines and yield increases.

### Question 34 (Average)

**Question:**
Nu's fund has a liability with present value 180,000 and modified duration of 4.0 years. Its current bond holdings are:

| Bond | Face amount held | Price per 100 of face value | Modified duration |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Xi | 100,000 | 96 | 2.5 years |
| Omicron | 80,000 | 105 | 7.0 years |

All durations use the same annual yield convention. Nu may transfer money between the two holdings at these prices, with no external cash and no short selling. Fractional face amounts are allowed.
a. Find the portfolio's current value and modified duration.
b. Determine the trade that matches the liability's present value and modified duration. State both the money transferred and the face amounts bought or sold.
c. Verify both matching conditions after the trade.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Immunization Conditions*:
  1. $V_{\text{assets}} = V_{\text{liability}}$
  2. $D^*_{\text{assets}} = D^*_{\text{liability}}$

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Current Portfolio Value and Duration:**
  - Current Market Value of Xi: $100{,}000 \times 0.96 = 96{,}000$ USD.
  - Current Market Value of Omicron: $80{,}000 \times 1.05 = 84{,}000$ USD.
  - Current Total Value $= 96{,}000 + 84{,}000 = \mathbf{180{,}000\text{ USD}}$ (Already equals liability PV!).
  - Current Portfolio Duration:
    $$D^*_P = \frac{96{,}000}{180{,}000}(2.5) + \frac{84{,}000}{180{,}000}(7.0) = \frac{240{,}000 + 588{,}000}{180{,}000} = \mathbf{4.60\text{ years}}$$
- **Part (b): Required Rebalancing Trade:**
  - Target duration is $4.0$ years. Let $w_{\text{Xi}}$ be Xi's target weight:
    $$w_{\text{Xi}} (2.5) + (1 - w_{\text{Xi}})(7.0) = 4.0$$
    $$7.0 - 4.5 w_{\text{Xi}} = 4.0 \implies 4.5 w_{\text{Xi}} = 3.0 \implies w_{\text{Xi}} = \frac{3}{4.5} = \mathbf{\frac{2}{3} \approx 0.6667}$$
    $$w_{\text{Omicron}} = 1 - \frac{2}{3} = \mathbf{\frac{1}{3} \approx 0.3333}$$
  - Target Market Values:
    - Target Xi: $180{,}000 \times \frac{2}{3} = 120{,}000$ USD.
    - Target Omicron: $180{,}000 \times \frac{1}{3} = 60{,}000$ USD.
  - Money to transfer:
    - **Buy Xi**: $120{,}000 - 96{,}000 = \mathbf{24{,}000\text{ USD}}$
    - **Sell Omicron**: $84{,}000 - 60{,}000 = \mathbf{24{,}000\text{ USD}}$
  - Face amounts traded:
    - Xi Face Bought: $\frac{24{,}000}{0.96} = \mathbf{25{,}000.00\text{ face amount}}$
    - Omicron Face Sold: $\frac{24{,}000}{1.05} = \mathbf{22{,}857.14\text{ face amount}}$
- **Part (c): Verification:**
  - $V_A = 120{,}000 + 60{,}000 = 180{,}000$ USD (Matches liability PV).
  - $D^*_A = \frac{2}{3}(2.5) + \frac{1}{3}(7.0) = 1.6667 + 2.3333 = 4.00$ years (Matches liability duration).

### Question 35 (Average)

**Question:**
Six months ago, Pi's fund matched the present value and duration of its coupon-bond assets to a single fixed liability. Since then, short-maturity yields have risen by 50 basis points and long-maturity yields have fallen by 25 basis points. The fund has not traded. Its manager proposes no review because the original durations matched.
a. Assess this proposal. What needs to be checked now?
b. Pi could instead afford a default-free, noncallable zero that pays exactly the liability amount on its due date, in the same currency. Would that replacement require the same rebalancing for rate changes? Explain.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Dynamic Nature of Immunization*: Duration matching is a local, instantaneous immunization against small, parallel shifts.
- *Cash-Flow Matching vs. Duration Matching*: Zero-coupon matching is bulletproof; coupon-bond duration matching decays with time and curve shape changes.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Assessment of Manager's Proposal:**
  - The proposal is **deeply flawed and unacceptable**.
  - **Reason 1 (Passage of Time)**: As time passes, bond durations and liability durations decay at different rates. Even with no change in yields, a portfolio of coupon bonds drifts away from duration matching and requires periodic rebalancing.
  - **Reason 2 (Non-Parallel Yield Curve Shift)**: The yield curve experienced a twisting/flattening shift (+50 bps short-end, -25 bps long-end). Duration matching only immunizes against **parallel** shifts. A curve twist alters the present values of assets and liabilities differently, potentially creating a serious funding deficit.
  - *What must be checked*: Pi must immediately reprice all assets and liabilities to check the funding ratio ($V_A / V_L$) and calculate current durations to rebalance the portfolio.
- **Part (b): Replacement with a Zero-Coupon Bond:**
  - **No**, the zero-coupon bond would **NOT require any rebalancing**.
  - A zero-coupon bond matching the exact date and currency of the liability is an **exact cash-flow match (dedication strategy)**. It has zero intermediate cash flows, zero reinvestment risk, and exactly pays off the liability upon maturity regardless of how interest rates move or how the yield curve shifts in the interim.

  - Weighted time sum:

### Question 37 (Hard)

**Question:**
Sigma is a straight bond; Tau has identical promised payments but can be called immediately for 1,075. Use this simplified model: Tau's value equals Sigma's value unless Sigma's value exceeds 1,075, in which case Tau is immediately called for 1,075. There is no accrued interest or call cost.
The table gives Sigma's values at each annual yield. At the initial 5% yield, Sigma's modified duration is 4.984990 years:

| Annual yield | Sigma value |
|---|---:|
| 4% | 1,104.842737 |
| 5% | 1,050.756921 |
| 6% | 1,000.000000 |

a. Find Tau's initial value and its value or redemption proceeds after each yield shock. Identify when it is called.
b. Calculate Tau's price change in USD and percent for each shock.
c. Use Sigma's initial duration to estimate prices after both shocks. Compare these estimates with Tau's outcomes and explain the limits of this approach.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Callable Bond Price*: $P_{\text{callable}} = \min(P_{\text{straight}}, \text{Call Price})$.
- *Negative Convexity*: At low yields, the call ceiling limits price gains, creating negative convexity where duration severely overestimates price increases.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Tau's Values and Call Status:**
  - *Initial at 5%*: $P_{\text{Sigma}} = 1{,}050.756921 < 1{,}075 \implies P_{\text{Tau}} = \mathbf{1{,}050.76\text{ USD}}$ (Not called).
  - *Yield falls to 4%*: $P_{\text{Sigma}} = 1{,}104.842737 > 1{,}075 \implies$ **Called!** Redemption proceeds $= \mathbf{1{,}075.00\text{ USD}}$.
  - *Yield rises to 6%*: $P_{\text{Sigma}} = 1{,}000.000000 < 1{,}075 \implies P_{\text{Tau}} = \mathbf{1{,}000.00\text{ USD}}$ (Not called).
- **Part (b): Tau's Price Changes:**
  - *Yield falls to 4% ($\Delta y = -1\%$)*:
    $$\Delta P = 1{,}075.00 - 1{,}050.756921 = \mathbf{+24.2431\text{ USD}} \quad \left(\frac{+24.2431}{1{,}050.7569} = \mathbf{+2.31\%}\right)$$
  - *Yield rises to 6% ($\Delta y = +1\%$)*:
    $$\Delta P = 1{,}000.00 - 1{,}050.756921 = \mathbf{-50.7569\text{ USD}} \quad \left(\frac{-50.7569}{1{,}050.7569} = \mathbf{-4.83\%}\right)$$

### Question 39 (Easy)

**Question:**

### Question 53 (Hard)

**Question:**
Eta forms an equally weighted portfolio of $n$ stocks. Every stock has annual SD of 30%, and the covariance between every distinct pair is 0.0100 in decimal-return units. These inputs remain unchanged as more stocks are added.
a. Find portfolio variance and SD for $n=10$.
b. Find the limiting SD as $n$ grows without bound.
c. What is the smallest whole number of stocks needed to obtain SD no greater than 12%?
d. Could adding stocks achieve an SD of 9% under these assumptions? Explain.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Equally Weighted Portfolio Variance*:
  $$\sigma_P^2 = \frac{1}{n}\bar{\sigma}^2 + \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)\overline{\text{Cov}} = \overline{\text{Cov}} + \frac{\bar{\sigma}^2 - \overline{\text{Cov}}}{n}$$
- With $\bar{\sigma}^2 = (0.30)^2 = 0.0900$ and $\overline{\text{Cov}} = 0.0100$:
  $$\sigma_P^2 = 0.0100 + \frac{0.0800}{n}$$

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): Variance and SD for $n=10$:**
  $$\sigma_P^2 = 0.0100 + \frac{0.0800}{10} = 0.0100 + 0.0080 = \mathbf{0.018000}$$
  $$\sigma_P = \sqrt{0.018000} = 0.134164 = \mathbf{13.4164\%}$$
- **Part (b): Limiting SD as $n 	o \infty$:**
  $$\lim_{n 	o \infty} \sigma_P^2 = \overline{\text{Cov}} = 0.010000$$
  $$\sigma_{\infty} = \sqrt{0.010000} = 0.1000 = \mathbf{10.00\%}$$
- **Part (c): Smallest Whole Number of Stocks for $\sigma_P \le 12\%$:**
  $$\sigma_P^2 \le (0.12)^2 = 0.014400$$
  $$0.0100 + \frac{0.0800}{n} \le 0.0144 \implies \frac{0.0800}{n} \le 0.0044 \implies n \ge \frac{0.0800}{0.0044} = 18.1818$$
  Rounding up to the next integer gives:
  $$\mathbf{n = 19\text{ stocks}}$$
- **Part (d): Can SD reach 9%?**
  - **No, it is impossible.**
  - As established in part (b), the theoretical lower limit of portfolio risk as $n 	o \infty$ is the systematic risk floor of $\sqrt{\overline{\text{Cov}}} = 10.00\%$. Diversification eliminates unique, firm-specific variance ($\frac{0.08}{n} \to 0$), but cannot eliminate the common systematic covariance shared across all stocks. An SD of $9\%$ is below the non-diversifiable risk limit.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *Tobin's Separation Theorem*: Portfolio choice separates into two independent tasks:
  1. Purely objective determination of the optimal risky portfolio $P$ (tangency portfolio maximizing Sharpe ratio).
  2. Subjective allocation between $P$ and the risk-free asset based on risk aversion $A$.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): What is Common vs. What Differs:**
  - **Common**: Both investors choose the **exact same risky portfolio $P$** (the same internal weights among risky assets). The composition of $P$ depends strictly on market inputs (expected returns, variances, covariances, $r_f$) and is independent of individual preferences.
  - **Different**: Their **allocation between $P$ and cash ($y^*$)** differs. Because $y^* = \frac{E(r_P) - r_f}{A \sigma_P^2} \propto \frac{1}{A}$:
    - Delta ($A=2$) is aggressive and invests 3 times as much into $P$ ($y^*_{\text{Delta}} = 3 y^*_{\text{Epsilon}}$), likely borrowing on margin.
    - Epsilon ($A=6$) is conservative, investing a smaller fraction in $P$ and lending cash.
- **Part (b): Impact of Borrowing Ban:**
  - **Epsilon's Portfolio**: **Does NOT change**. Because Epsilon has high risk aversion ($A=6$), Epsilon's optimal risky weight is already well below 1 ($y^* < 1$), meaning Epsilon was already a lender. A borrowing ban is non-binding for Epsilon.
  - **Delta's Portfolio**: **CHANGES**. Delta's unconstrained choice required leverage ($y^* > 1$). Banning borrowing forces Delta to an upper bound of $y = 1.0$ (or alters the optimal risky portfolio along the Markowitz frontier without a risk-free asset).

### Question 83 (Average)

**Question:**
A historical regression estimates Phi's monthly excess return as:
$$\hat{R}_{\mathrm{Phi},t}=0.003+1.2R_{M,t}.$$
Phi has nonzero residual variance. Assess each claim:
a. “The positive fitted intercept disproves CAPM and establishes a positive future alpha.”
b. “If Phi is correctly priced, it belongs on the capital market line (CML) at the horizontal coordinate 1.2.”
c. “A positive forecast alpha would guarantee a risk-free profit from buying Phi and hedging its market beta.”

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *CML vs. SML*: CML graphs Return vs. Total Risk $\sigma$ (holds only for efficient complete portfolios). SML graphs Return vs. Systematic Risk $\beta$ (holds for all securities).
- *Alpha & Arbitrage*: Pure arbitrage requires zero net risk ($\sigma = 0$). Hedging beta leaves unhedged residual risk ($\sigma_e > 0$).

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Claim (a):** **Incorrect.**

- **Claim (b):** **Incorrect.**
  - The Capital Market Line (CML) plots expected return against **total risk ($\sigma$)**, not beta. Only efficient complete portfolios lie on the CML; individual stocks with firm-specific risk lie strictly **below** the CML. The line that plots return against beta (at coordinate $1.2$) is the **Security Market Line (SML)**.
- **Claim (c):** **Incorrect.**
  - Buying Phi and shorting $1.2$ units of the market eliminates market systematic risk, but leaves the position fully exposed to **Phi's residual risk ($\sigma_e > 0$)**. Because firm-specific shocks can cause severe losses, this strategy is an active bet, not a risk-free arbitrage.

### Question 90 (Easy)

**Question:**
Assess the following claims:
a. Tau: “APT needs the same efficient market portfolio and mean–variance investor assumptions as CAPM.”

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- *CAPM vs. APT Foundations*:
  - CAPM: Relies on mean-variance optimization, normality, and unobservable all-inclusive market portfolio.

- **Part (b): Upsilon's Claim & What APT Leaves Unspecified:**
  - **Upsilon's Claim is Incorrect**: Adding any explanatory variable mechanically inflates in-sample $R^2$ even if it is completely spurious. A factor earns a risk premium in equilibrium **only if it proxies for pervasive, non-diversifiable macroeconomic risk** that risk-averse investors require compensation to bear.
  - **What APT leaves unspecified**:
    APT provides no theoretical guidance on:
    1. **What the factors are** (macro variables vs. firm characteristics).
    2. **How many factors exist** ($K=1, 3, 5\dots$).
    3. **The sign and magnitude of the factor risk premia** ($\lambda_k$).

  - **Does it track Factor 1 without error?** **NO.** Although its expected sensitivity matches Factor 1 ($\beta_{P,1} = 1.0$) and neutralizes Factor 2 ($\beta_{P,2} = 0.0$), the portfolio carries a large residual standard deviation ($\sigma_e = 12.02\%$). Actual returns will deviate substantially from Factor 1 due to firm-specific shocks.
  - **Is it risk-free?** **NO.** The portfolio is exposed to both systematic Factor 1 risk ($\sigma = 16\%$) and idiosyncratic residual risk ($\sigma = 12.02\%$), resulting in a total volatility of $20.01\%$.

### Question 100 (Hard)

**Question:**
Rho and Sigma are evaluated using the same 60 monthly observations. Fund returns are after fees. The sample arithmetic means are:
- Market excess return: 0.60%
- SMB: 0.20%
- HML: 0.30%
- Risk-free return: 0.20%
- Rho total return: 1.20% (Excess = 1.00%)
- Sigma total return: 1.08% (Excess = 0.88%)
Regressions report:
- Rho: CAPM $eta = 1.00$; FF3 $eta_M = 0.90, eta_{	ext{SMB}} = 0.80, eta_{	ext{HML}} = 0.60$.
- Sigma: CAPM $eta = 0.95$; FF3 $eta_M = 1.00, eta_{	ext{SMB}} = -0.20, eta_{	ext{HML}} = 0.10$.
a. Calculate each fund's zero-alpha benchmark total return and monthly alpha under CAPM.
b. Repeat under FF3.
c. Reconcile the change in each fund's alpha using the changes in market, size, and value contributions.
d. Rank the funds by estimated alpha under each model. Explain what the comparison shows about performance assessment.

**Key Concepts & Formulas (Khái niệm then chốt & Công thức):**
- CAPM Benchmark: $r_{\text{bench}} = r_f + \beta_M R_M$.
- FF3 Benchmark: $r_{\text{bench}} = r_f + \beta_M R_M + \beta_{\text{SMB}} \text{SMB} + \beta_{\text{HML}} \text{HML}$.
- Alpha: $\alpha = r_{\text{actual}} - r_{\text{bench}}$.

**Detailed Solution & Explanation (Lời giải chi tiết & Phân tích chuyên sâu):**
- **Part (a): CAPM Benchmarks and Alphas:**
  - **Fund Rho ($r = 1.20\%, R = 1.00\%$):**
    - Benchmark excess: $1.00 \times 0.60\% = 0.60\%$.
    - Benchmark total: $0.20\% + 0.60\% = \mathbf{0.80\%}$.
    - CAPM Alpha: $\alpha_{\text{CAPM}} = 1.20\% - 0.80\% = \mathbf{+0.40\%}$ ($40$ bps/month).
  - **Fund Sigma ($r = 1.08\%, R = 0.88\%$):**
    - Benchmark excess: $0.95 \times 0.60\% = 0.57\%$.
    - Benchmark total: $0.20\% + 0.57\% = \mathbf{0.77\%}$.
    - CAPM Alpha: $\alpha_{\text{CAPM}} = 1.08\% - 0.77\% = \mathbf{+0.31\%}$ ($31$ bps/month).
- **Part (b): FF3 Benchmarks and Alphas:**
  - **Fund Rho:**
    - Benchmark excess:
      $$R_{\text{bench, FF3}} = 0.90(0.60\%) + 0.80(0.20\%) + 0.60(0.30\%) = 0.54\% + 0.16\% + 0.18\% = 0.88\%$$
    - Benchmark total: $0.20\% + 0.88\% = \mathbf{1.08\%}$.
    - FF3 Alpha: $\alpha_{\text{FF3}} = 1.20\% - 1.08\% = \mathbf{+0.12\%}$ ($12$ bps/month).
  - **Fund Sigma:**
    - Benchmark excess:
      $$R_{\text{bench, FF3}} = 1.00(0.60\%) - 0.20(0.20\%) + 0.10(0.30\%) = 0.60\% - 0.04\% + 0.03\% = 0.59\%$$
    - Benchmark total: $0.20\% + 0.59\% = \mathbf{0.79\%}$.
    - FF3 Alpha: $\alpha_{\text{FF3}} = 1.08\% - 0.79\% = \mathbf{+0.29\%}$ ($29$ bps/month).
- **Part (c): Reconciliation of Alpha Changes:**
  - **Rho**:
    $$\Delta \alpha = \alpha_{\text{FF3}} - \alpha_{\text{CAPM}} = +0.12\% - 0.40\% = \mathbf{-0.28\%}$$
    - Explanation: Rho loaded heavily on Small-Cap ($SMB=0.80$, contributing $+0.16\%$) and Value ($HML=0.60$, contributing $+0.18\%$). These factor tilts contributed $+0.34\%$ of passive return, offset by a slightly lower market beta ($0.90$ vs $1.00$, saving $-0.06\%$). The net benchmark increase of $+0.28\%$ explains the exact drop in alpha from $0.40\%$ to $0.12\%$.
  - **Sigma**:
    $$\Delta \alpha = \alpha_{\text{FF3}} - \alpha_{\text{CAPM}} = +0.29\% - 0.31\% = \mathbf{-0.02\%}$$
    - Explanation: Sigma had negligible net size and value exposure ($-0.04\% + 0.03\% = -0.01\%$) and a market beta shift of $+0.05$ (adding $+0.03\%$). Its benchmark barely changed ($0.77\% \to 0.79\%$).
- **Part (d): Rankings and Fundamental Lessons:**
  - **CAPM Ranking**: **Rho (1st, 0.40%) > Sigma (2nd, 0.31%)**.
  - **FF3 Ranking**: **Sigma (1st, 0.29%) > Rho (2nd, 0.12%)**.
  - **Key Lesson for Performance Assessment**:
    The ranking **reverses** between the two models!
    Under CAPM, Rho appeared to be the superior manager. However, FF3 reveals that Rho's apparent "alpha" was largely an illusion created by passive factor tilts toward small-cap and value stocks (which earned positive market premia). Once factor exposures are appropriately controlled for, Sigma demonstrates far greater genuine stock-picking skill ($0.29\%$ true alpha vs $0.12\%$). Multi-factor models are critical to avoid paying active management fees for passive factor exposures.
